# Project Philosophy

## Human purpose before technological capability

This project begins with a human question, not an AI question: how can technology improve protection, support, and family reunification without increasing the ability of institutions or machines to control vulnerable people?

The project adopts the ethical intuition that protecting even one human life or preserving one person's dignity is sufficient reason to take system design seriously. Technology is therefore treated as a means, never as the moral center of the system.

## Power is the central design problem

A secure database is not enough. Legitimate credentials, administrative authority, legal authority, analytics, and institutional power can all be misused even when no hacker is present.

The architecture therefore treats concentration of informational power as a first-class threat.

> The system should give every actor enough information and authority to perform a legitimate task, but no more.

## Data is about a person, not merely an asset

A record about a child or vulnerable family is not ordinary inventory. It may encode relationships, health, trauma, location, identity, or future decisions.

The project therefore favors minimization, compartmentalization, selective disclosure, revocability, and purpose limitation over unrestricted collection.

## AI is not the moral agent

AI can accelerate search, pattern recognition, summarization, comparison, and evidence retrieval. It does not inherit moral responsibility from the human professionals who use it.

Accordingly:

> AI may assist the decision. AI must not own the decision.

The social worker or other accountable professional remains responsible for the professional judgment. High-impact decisions must remain reviewable and appealable.

## Consent is not a fingerprint

Authentication proves who performed an action. It does not prove that a person understood an explanation, was free from coercion, or had the legal and cognitive capacity to consent.

For that reason, biometric activation is separated from consent. Consent requires an understandable explanation, purpose, scope, expiry, and the ability to refuse or revoke where appropriate.

## Distributed trust over institutional goodwill

The project does not assume that any government, company, administrator, provider, social worker, judge, or AI will always act correctly.

It attempts to make abuse harder by distributing knowledge and authority so that no single actor can independently reconstruct and control the whole person.

## Open knowledge

This is an individual public-interest research project. Its architecture, assumptions, limitations, threat model, and lessons are intentionally documented in formats readable by both humans and machines.

The purpose of publication is not to claim a final answer. It is to make the design inspectable, criticizable, reproducible, and improvable.

## Limits

Technology cannot guarantee protection against every lawful, institutional, political, or coercive use of power. This project does not claim otherwise.

The realistic goal is narrower but meaningful: make mass access, silent access, unnecessary access, single-person access, and unaccountable access more difficult and more visible.

## Core question

> How can increasingly capable AI help humans care for vulnerable humans without allowing informational power to exceed human responsibility, rights, and safeguards?
